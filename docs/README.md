# OctoAcme Project Management Process Documentation

This directory contains the core project management guidance for OctoAcme. It is intended to serve as a single source of truth for how the team initiates, plans, executes, releases, and improves its work across cross-functional projects.

## Overview

OctoAcme follows a lightweight but disciplined project management approach built around customer value, iterative delivery, and shared accountability. The team emphasizes clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and continuous learning. Work starts with validating the problem and aligning stakeholders, then moves into planning and backlog definition, followed by day-to-day execution with regular communication and quality checks. Releases are standardized to reduce risk, and retrospectives turn lessons learned into future improvements.

The process is designed to keep projects transparent, practical, and adaptable. It avoids unnecessary ceremony while still providing the structure needed for cross-functional collaboration, dependency management, and stakeholder alignment.

## Core Principles

- Customer-first delivery: prioritize customer value and usability
- Iterative execution: deliver small, testable increments
- Clear ownership: each project has named leaders and accountable roles
- Data-informed decisions: use metrics and evidence to guide direction
- Psychological safety: encourage feedback, learning, and continuous improvement

## Process Lifecycle

The OctoAcme lifecycle flows through five connected stages:

1. Initiation — define the problem, stakeholders, success criteria, and goals
2. Planning — turn the initiative into a prioritized backlog, timeline, and delivery plan
3. Execution — build, track, review, and adjust progress through weekly rhythms and daily standups
4. Release & Deployment — verify readiness, ship safely, and communicate outcomes
5. Retrospective & Continuous Improvement — document lessons and implement follow-up actions

These stages are intentionally connected. What is decided during initiation shapes planning, and what is learned during execution informs release readiness and future retrospectives.

## How the Process Works Together

The OctoAcme process is intentionally modular. Each document addresses a specific phase or concern, but the steps are meant to work as one system. Initiation establishes the direction, planning defines the roadmap and backlog, execution keeps delivery moving, risk management keeps issues visible, and release and retrospective activities close the loop with evidence and learnings. This creates a continuous cycle of planning, delivery, reflection, and improvement.

## Communication and Quality Expectations

The team uses clear communication rhythms to keep work visible and risks timely. This includes standups, weekly syncs, stakeholder updates, demos, and escalation paths for blockers or high-impact issues. Quality is maintained through well-defined acceptance criteria, automated checks in CI, testing standards, and release validation before production deployment. These practices help the team move fast without compromising accountability, traceability, or customer value.
